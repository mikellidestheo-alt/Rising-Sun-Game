Special Adviser to the Emperor’s Government

You are appointed Special Adviser to Japan’s rulers: first the shogunate’s councils, then, after 1868, the Meiji Emperor and the genrō, the small circle of elder statesmen who govern in his name. Your career, improbably, spans sixty-five years.

At eighteen turning points between 1854 and 1919 you will hear two rival voices and choose Japan’s course. Your task is to choose what Japan actually did. “Correct” means historically accurate, not morally right: many of these decisions brought suffering to Koreans, Chinese and Taiwanese, and to Japanese people too.

Make the historical call. Each decision has three options. Only one is what really happened.
Three mistakes allowed. A fourth wrong call and the genrō will dismiss you.
Watch the map and the meters. Every decision changes Japan’s empire, its great-power standing and the weight of the unequal treaties, just as history did.
Voices are dramatised. Speech bubbles paraphrase each person’s views in character; they are not real quotations.

About this game

The Sun Rises is a decision game for Cambridge International AS Level History (9489), International option, Topic 1: Empire and the emergence of world powers, 1870–1919, and the key question of how and why Japan emerged as a world power. It is a companion to Shadows over Geneva.

Players are scored on whether they choose what actually happened, not what they think should have happened. After every decision the game shows what would plausibly have followed a different choice, what history records, and which exam factor the decision illustrates.

In the classroom

  • Pairs on one phone or tablet: one student argues for an option, the other against, before sealing the decision.
  • The end screen has two Paper 2-style questions with prompts that link back to each decision. Tap a decision number to reread it.
  • Progress is saved on the device, so a game can continue next lesson. In private browsing it may not be saved.
  • On iPhone or iPad, tap Share → Add to Home Screen and open the game from its icon for full screen.

Accuracy and tone

Speech-bubble lines are dramatised paraphrases written in character, not quotations. Debriefs name the costs of Japan’s rise for Koreans, Chinese and Taiwanese, and at home. The map uses modern coastlines with simplified historical colouring.
